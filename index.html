Design Patterns

What are design patterns?

Design patterns are reusable solutions to problems that come up repeatedly in software design. They're not finished code — they're templates you adapt to your situation. The term was popularised by the "Gang of Four" book Design Patterns (1994).

Three categories

  • Creational — control how objects are created (e.g. Singleton, Factory).
  • Structural — organise relationships between objects (e.g. Adapter, Decorator).
  • Behavioural — manage communication between objects (e.g. Observer, Strategy).

Why bother?

Patterns give teams a shared vocabulary. Saying "use an Observer here" conveys more than a paragraph of description. They also encode hard-won lessons about what tends to go wrong when code is structured carelessly.

How to use this site
